2. Problem Statement
Rural and small-scale retailers in India face multiple challenges:
•	High-Cost Credit: Many retailers rely on money lenders who charge exorbitant interest rates (50-100% annually) due to limited access to formal banking services.
•	Logistical Barriers: Retailers must often travel long distances to replenish their stock, leading to business closures for hours or days, especially due to limited FMCG distributor networks in rural areas.
•	No Access to Formal Credit: Traditional banks often reject small retailers’ loan applications, leaving them with limited options.
•	Limited or No Credit Period from Distributors: Distributors often do not offer flexible credit terms, impacting retailers' cash flow.
•	Inefficient Inventory Management: Retailers lack digital tools for inventory tracking, stock forecasting, or financial management, leading to stock-outs and lost sales opportunities.
•	Lack of Investment Instruments: Retailers with surplus funds have no proper channels to invest for better returns.

Growing up in a small town, my world often revolved around my father's shop. It was more than just a source of income; it was the lifeline of our family. I remember the long hours my father spent behind the counter, greeting customers with a warm smile, while silently carrying the weight of his responsibilities.
There were days when my father needed to travel to the nearby town to restock goods, but this meant either leaving the shop closed or asking my mother, sister, or me to take his place. Closing the shop, even for a few hours, wasn’t an option—it would mean losing customers and income. And yet, sending us to school or doing household errands while juggling the shop seemed impossible.
What struck me the most was the quiet sacrifices my parents made. I never saw them taking a day off or enjoying simple pleasures like a family outing. Their dedication to keeping the shop running was admirable, but it came at a cost—moments of joy and freedom they never got to experience.
Then there was the financial strain. My father often had to take loans from local moneylenders at exorbitant interest rates—10% a month. I saw his worry grow as he worked tirelessly to pay off these debts, with little left to improve the shop or save for the future.
These memories stayed with me. Every time I saw another shopkeeper in our community facing similar struggles, I realized this wasn’t just my family’s story—it was the story of countless others. Families working hard to make ends meet, but held back by outdated systems, lack of access to fair credit, and the burden of managing everything alone.
DukaaOn was born from this experience. It’s not just a business idea; it’s a heartfelt mission to help shopkeepers like my father. I want to make sure no family has to struggle as we did, that shopkeepers can access fair credit, manage their stock efficiently, and spend more time with their loved ones without the constant burden of running a business. DukaaOn is my way of giving back to the community that shaped me, by ensuring their businesses, their dreams, and their families can keep going on.
